# Desk research — Little Joys flavour-choice experience

**Date checked:** 12 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public product information and reviews. This is not the five-customer research required for the challenge.

## Signals observed

1. Little Joys offers a NutriMix three-flavour trial pack. The official product page positions tasting multiple flavours as useful when a child is fussy and says some parents want a chance to taste before buying larger packs.
2. Reviews on that page mention a parent having wasted two nutrition powders before finding a flavour, and another parent buying flavours because their child gets bored with the same one.
3. A Little Joys App Store review describes children liking a range of its products; another describes asking support to clarify an ingredient appearance. The app reviews also include order and packaging complaints, suggesting multiple possible problem areas.

## Working problem hypothesis

Some Little Joys NutriMix shoppers may be uncertain which flavour their child will accept and may have to learn through trial and error. A small tasting journal could help a family compare its own experience and make its next flavour choice with more confidence.

## Why this is only a hypothesis

- Reviews are self-selected and do not establish frequency or representativeness.
- The official trial pack already addresses part of this need; interviews must reveal whether a digital helper adds value beyond the pack itself.
- “Preference tracking” may be unnecessary, annoying, or inappropriate for families. The interviews could show the real issue is price, access, ingredients, preparation, delivery, support, or something else.
- The prototype must be revised to match what at least five real customer conversations show.

## Interview plan

Interview five adult Little Joys customers. Ask which products and flavours they personally used, how they chose, what happened when they tried a new flavour, what they did when the child rejected or got bored of a product, what cost or waste meant to them, and what would make choosing easier. Ask about the broader journey (discovery, purchase, trial, repeat order, support). Do not lead with the proposed app. Obtain explicit consent before recording and sharing actual names, phone numbers, and feedback in the application. Never put their identity or personal details in the app.
